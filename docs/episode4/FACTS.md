## Part 1 — The animal fact and why it happens

| # | Claim as narrated | Source(s) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emperor penguins are the largest penguins, standing more than a meter tall (about 1.1–1.2 m) and weighing about 22–45 kg depending on the season. | Australian Antarctic Program (AAP), *Emperor penguin* | ✅ The weight swings a lot through the fasting cycle, hence "depending on the season". |
| 2 | They are the deepest-diving birds, reaching more than 500 m. | AAP, *Emperor penguin* | ✅ The record dives in the literature are about 550–560 m; the script says "more than 500". |
| 3 | They are the only penguins that breed in the Antarctic winter. | British Antarctic Survey (BAS), *Emperor penguin* | ✅ |
| 4 | Why winter: the chick takes so long to grow that an egg laid as winter begins gives a chick ready for sea in summer, when food is easier to find. | BAS; AAP | ✅ Phrased as the timing explanation the agencies give. |
| 5 | In 2022, early sea-ice loss in one region wiped out almost all chicks in four of five colonies there. | Fretwell, Boutet & Ratcliffe 2023, *Communications Earth & Environment* 4:273 | ✅ It was the central/eastern Bellingshausen Sea region, and the paper says "likely". The script says "one region of Antarctica". |
| 6 | The mother lays a single egg and passes it to the father's feet; an egg left on bare ice can freeze. | AAP; National Geographic | ✅ / ⚠️ How fast an exposed egg freezes isn't stated precisely in the sources, so no time is narrated. |
| 7 | The mother goes back to sea to feed for about two months. | AAP; National Geographic | ✅ |
| 8 | The father holds the egg in a feathered fold of belly skin (brood pouch) on his feet for about 65 days. | AAP (≈64–67 days); National Geographic | ✅ |
| 9 | Counting the walk to the colony and courtship, the father can go about 115 days without food. | AAP | ✅ |
| 10 | …and lose up to around 40% of his body weight. | Groscolas 1986, *J. Comp. Physiol. B* 156:521; AAP | ⚠️ Published losses are roughly 35–45% depending on study and year, so the script says "up to around 40 percent". |
| 11 | Thousands of males huddle; inside, the temperature can climb above 20 °C. | Gilbert et al. 2006, *Physiology & Behavior* 88:479 (above 20 °C, up to 37.5 °C) | ✅ |
| 12 | Huddling saves a lot of energy and helps them last through the fast. | Ancel et al. 1997, *Nature* 385:304; Gilbert et al. 2006 | ✅ |
| 13 | Every 30–60 seconds the huddle shuffles forward a few centimeters in a wave; over time penguins move between the edge and the middle. | Zitterbart et al. 2011, *PLoS ONE* 6:e20260; Gilbert et al. 2006 | ✅ "A few centimeters" is how the small steps are described. | 14 | When it gets too warm, the huddle breaks apart for a while. | Gilbert et al. 2006 (huddles are unstable and short-lived) | ✅ |
| 15 | If the chick hatches before the mother returns, the father can feed it a rich, milky secretion from his esophagus ("penguin milk"). | AAP; National Geographic | ✅ "Penguin milk" is a nickname, said with "sometimes called". |
| 16 | When the mother returns, the parents swap and the father walks to the sea; the walk can be dozens of kilometers. | AAP | ✅ Distances vary by colony and ice conditions, so no exact number is narrated. |

## Part 3 — Lab ending and teaser

- "Bix lasted eleven minutes, then got a sandwich" and "Newton is now raising the egg" are jokes, not claims.
- The teaser for episode 5: "what if you had three hearts and blue blood, like an octopus?"

## Deliberately left out

- **Exact temperatures and wind speeds** at the colonies: quoted values vary a lot, and some sources mix air temperature with wind chill.
- **Egg weight and exact incubation temperature:** I couldn't confirm a single reliable figure.
- **"Penguins mate for life":** emperor penguins usually take a new partner each year (a popular myth).
