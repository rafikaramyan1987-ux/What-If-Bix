- Australian Antarctic Program (Australian Government), Emperor penguin — largest penguin (about 1.1–1.2 m, ~22–45 kg depending on season), winter breeding, single egg passed to the male, ~64–67-day incubation on the feet, males fast for up to ~115 days, chick feeding from an oesophageal secretion, long walks to open water, dives deeper than 500 m: https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-antarctica/animals/penguins/emperor-penguin/
- Fretwell, P. T., Boutet, A. & Ratcliffe, N. (2023). Record low 2022 Antarctic sea ice led to catastrophic breeding failure of emperor penguins. Communications Earth & Environment 4: 273 — four of five colonies in the central/eastern Bellingshausen Sea region likely lost almost all chicks: https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-023-00927-x
- British Antarctic Survey, Emperor penguin — the only penguin species that breeds during the Antarctic winter; chicks fledge in summer: https://www.bas.ac.uk/about/antarctica/wildlife/penguins/emperor-penguin/
- National Geographic, Emperor penguin — egg transferred to the male's feet and brood pouch; females feed at sea about two months; eggs exposed on the ice can freeze: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/birds/facts/emperor-penguin
- Groscolas, R. (1986). Changes in body mass, body temperature and plasma fuel levels during the natural breeding fast in male and female emperor penguins. Journal of Comparative Physiology B 156: 521–527 — large body-mass loss (roughly 40%) over the males' long breeding fast: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00691038
- Ancel, A., Visser, H., Handrich, Y., Masman, D. & Le Maho, Y. (1997). Energy saving in huddling penguins. Nature 385: 304–305: https://doi.org/10.1038/385304a0
- Gilbert, C., Robertson, G., Le Maho, Y., Naito, Y. & Ancel, A. (2006). Huddling behavior in emperor penguins: dynamics of huddling. Physiology & Behavior 88(4–5): 479–488 — ambient temperature inside huddles above 20 °C (up to 37.5 °C); huddles are unstable and break up regularly: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2006.04.024
- Zitterbart, D. P., Wienecke, B., Butler, J. P. & Fabry, B. (2011). Coordinated movements prevent jamming in an emperor penguin huddle. PLoS ONE 6(6): e20260 — small steps every 30–60 s travel through the huddle as waves: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0020260
- U.S. National Weather Service, Wind chill chart — at extreme wind chills, frostbite can occur on exposed skin in 5 minutes or less: https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart
- Peel, M. (1997). Hunger strikes. BMJ 315: 829–830 — death from total starvation typically occurs after about 45–75 days: https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.315.7112.829
- Gettler, L. T., McDade, T. W., Feranil, A. B. & Kuzawa, C. W. (2011). Longitudinal evidence that fatherhood decreases testosterone in human males. PNAS 108(39): 16194–16199 (Cebu, Philippines; larger drop with more childcare): https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1105403108
- Stölting, K. N. & Wilson, A. B. (2007). Male pregnancy in seahorses and pipefish: beyond the mammalian model. BioEssays 29(9): 884–896 — males carry embryos in a brood pouch; brood sizes range up to hundreds or more depending on species: https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.20626
