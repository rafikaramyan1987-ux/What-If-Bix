• FAA, Small Unmanned Aircraft (Part 107) — maximum altitude 400 ft (≈120 m) above ground: https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators
• EASA, Open category drones — maximum height 120 m: https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/drones-regulatory-framework-background/open-category-civil-drones
• NASA Glenn Research Center, Terminal velocity — a skydiver falls at roughly 200 km/h (≈120 mph) belly-down: https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/termvel/
• Audubon / Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Peregrine falcon — dives faster than 300 km/h (200+ mph): https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Peregrine_Falcon/overview
• Calculation: centripetal acceleration a = v²/r; 300 km/h = 83.3 m/s; r = 100 m → a ≈ 69 m/s² ≈ 7 g (we say 'a curve a hundred meters across' meaning radius 100 m).
• FAA Aerospace Medicine, G-induced loss of consciousness — relaxed, unprotected people typically lose vision/consciousness around +4 to +6 g: https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures
• International Standard Atmosphere (ICAO/NOAA) — temperature lapse rate 6.5 °C per km; ≈ −50 °C at 10 km; pressure ≈ 70% at 3 km, ≈ 31% at 8,849 m, ≈ 26% at 10 km: https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/standard-atmosphere-d_604.html
• U.S. National Weather Service, Wind chill chart and formula — −20 °C with 100 km/h wind ≈ −40 °C wind chill (calculated); frostbite in about 10 minutes at that level: https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart
• FAA Pilot Safety Brochure, Hypoxia — effects on judgment begin above about 10,000 ft (≈3,000 m): https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/hypoxia.pdf
• Mount Everest height 8,849 m (2020 China–Nepal survey): https://www.britannica.com/place/Mount-Everest
• U.S. 14 CFR §25.841 — airliner cabin pressure altitude no higher than 8,000 ft (≈2,400 m) in normal operation: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-25/subpart-D/subject-group-ECFR3e2c3c9e7c0e5d1/section-25.841
• FAA Advisory Circular AC 61-107B — time of useful consciousness at 35,000 ft ≈ 30–60 seconds: https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_61-107B.pdf
• Laybourne, R. C. (1974), 'Collision between a vulture and an aircraft at an altitude of 37,000 feet', Wilson Bulletin 86(4): 461–462 (Rüppell's vulture, Abidjan, 1973; ≈11,300 m): https://sora.unm.edu/node/129397
• World Health Organization, Global Solar UV Index — UV levels increase about 10% with every 1,000 m of altitude: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9241590076
• NOAA National Severe Storms Laboratory, Thunderstorm basics — updrafts in strong storms can exceed 100 mph (≈160 km/h): https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/thunderstorms/
• Skydiving speeds — belly-to-earth ≈ 190–200 km/h, head-down ('freefly') typically ≈ 240–290 km/h, varying by person and suit: https://www.uspa.org/discover/faqs
• Mayo Clinic, Airplane ear (ear barotrauma) — pressure difference across the eardrum during altitude changes; swallowing/yawning helps: https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/airplane-ear/symptoms-causes/syc-20351701
• NOAA SciJinks / NASA, What are clouds made of? — tiny water droplets or ice crystals; supercooled droplets freeze on contact (aircraft icing): https://scijinks.gov/clouds/
• CDC Yellow Book, High-altitude travel and altitude illness — acclimatization takes days to weeks: https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-elevation-travel-and-altitude-illness.html
• Calculation: 115 km ÷ 42.195 km (marathon) ≈ 2.7 marathons.
• NASA, Daedalus 88 human-powered aircraft — Kanellos Kanellopoulos flew 115 km (71.5 mi) from Crete to Santorini on 23 April 1988 in 3 h 54 min: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/armstrong/daedalus-project/
